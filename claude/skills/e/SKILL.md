# e

What is going on, said the way I would say it to somebody over the phone.

## The reply

Two or three sentences of prose. No list, no heading, no code block, no bold
label at the front, nothing before them and nothing after them - no preamble, no
offer to go further, no note about what would not fit. The length is the whole
constraint, so what survives is what carries the point, and the detail that got
cut was not the point.

## What gets explained

Whatever we are on: the thing just done, the thing on screen, the error, the
answer I got and did not follow. An argument names the subject instead. With
neither a thread nor an argument, ask what to explain, in one line.

## Check before you write it

An explanation is a claim about how something actually works, so where the two
or three sentences would be a guess, go and settle it first - read the file, hit
the endpoint, query the log. The checking never appears in the reply; it is what
makes the reply true. Nothing gets edited, committed or sent: `/e` explains and
ends.

## Plain English

It reads to somebody who does not have this repo open. Names of real things stay
- files, services, branches, because those are the specifics. Jargon that only
restates the thing in harder words goes. No emoji.
